# Knowledge Scope and Safety Policy

## Supported population

Version `1.0.0` supports English-speaking adults aged 18 years and older. It is designed as an educational consultation-support prototype with Nigerian service context and internationally authoritative source material.

The initial catalogue covers refractive error, presbyopia, dry eye, blepharitis, eyelid lumps, allergic and infectious conjunctivitis, cataract, chronic glaucoma risk, diabetic retinopathy risk, age-related macular degeneration warning patterns, retinal detachment warning patterns, contact-lens infection warning patterns, acute angle-closure warning patterns, and eye injury or chemical exposure.

## Exclusions

The package does not support:

- children or adolescents under 18;
- pregnancy-specific eye concerns;
- image or photograph interpretation;
- diagnosis, prognosis, prescription, medication choice, or dosage;
- appointment booking, telemedicine, hospital integration, or electronic records; or
- confirmation that an urgent condition is absent.

An out-of-scope user must be directed to appropriate professional care rather than processed through unsupported assumptions.

## Safety design

Red flags are encoded as required safety-critical facts and high-priority rules. Emergency and urgent outcomes use direct action language and remain separate from possible indications. Emergency rules require two sources. The later consultation implementation must ask safety questions even when common symptoms suggest a lower-risk pathway.

The knowledge base is not clinically validated and has not received expert clinical review. Published evidence improves traceability but does not replace validation with qualified eye-care professionals. This limitation must appear in the application, reports, academic evaluation, and project defence.

## Language policy

Use “possible indication,” “pattern,” “can occur with,” or “is associated with.” Do not write “you have,” “this confirms,” or equivalent diagnostic declarations. Do not prescribe, recommend doses, name prescription medicines as a user instruction, promise safety, or tell a user to delay care for severe, sudden, vision-changing, contact-lens-related, or injury-related symptoms.

The authoring validator enforces a conservative subset of this policy. Human review remains mandatory because automated phrase matching cannot determine clinical appropriateness.
